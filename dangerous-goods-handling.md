# Dangerous Goods Handling Procedure

**Company:** Meridian Freight & Logistics (fictional)
**Document ID:** MFL-HSE-005 | **Version:** 2.1 | **Owner:** Dangerous Goods Safety Adviser (DGSA)

## 1. Scope
This procedure covers receiving, storing and dispatching dangerous goods (DG) at Meridian sites. It applies to road (ADR), sea (IMDG) and air (IATA) shipments. It is an internal summary and does not replace the regulations.

## 2. Acceptance
Meridian only accepts DG when:
- the customer has supplied a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) no older than 3 years
- the goods are correctly classified, packed, marked and labelled
- a Dangerous Goods Note or Shipper's Declaration is supplied

Meridian does **not** accept Class 1 (explosives) or Class 7 (radioactive) goods at any site.

## 3. Storage and segregation
- DG are stored only in the designated DG cage or bunded area.
- Flammable liquids (Class 3) are never stored next to oxidisers (Class 5.1).
- Lithium batteries (UN3480, UN3481) are stored away from heat sources, with a maximum stack height of 1.5 m.
- Spill kits must be within 10 m of every DG storage area and checked weekly.

## 4. Training
Staff who handle DG complete DG awareness training before they start and refresh it every 2 years. Staff who prepare air shipments need IATA DGR training, refreshed every 24 months.

## 5. Spills and leaks
1. Stop work and keep people away.
2. Check the SDS for hazards and the right spill response.
3. Use the spill kit only if trained and it is safe to do so.
4. Inform the Shift Supervisor and the DGSA.
5. Record the incident in the HSE portal.

## 6. Dispatch
The driver's vehicle must show the correct orange plates for ADR loads. Drivers must hold a valid ADR certificate. The transport planner checks load compatibility before releasing the vehicle.
